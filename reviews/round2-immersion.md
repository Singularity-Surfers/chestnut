VERDICT: NOT SATISFIED

**Score: 5 / 10** for immersion and art direction (round 1 was about 4). Role: `immersion`, round 2.

The build is clearly better than round 1: the shadow stipple is gone, still water now reflects the forest, the tone of the hero view has come down to the painting's key, the far field is no longer dragon scales, and the leaf litter is good enough that the uncurated spots hold together at ground level. But it still does not make me feel I am standing among ancient chestnuts. The giants are wide but read as props: smooth cones on a tent skirt with painted stripes for bark, never seen from root to first limb, and the hero tree wears a ring of foliage round its waist. The palette is pure chroma with almost no neutrals (lemon yellow, crimson, candy pink, lime), so it reads as a stylised game rather than a southern Appalachian October. Eye level is crowded and evenly planted, which hides the big trees the place is about. And "vary the weather, time of day" is where it breaks hardest: rain and after-rain turn every trunk chalk white, dusk is a black frame, dawn and late afternoon look like noon, and the stream turns white at golden hour. The Richards homage now has the cast in the right places but not the light.

Reviewed at full size: all 12 of `shots/set7`, all 16 of `shots/var7`, 22 portraits (`habit_chestnut*`, `habit_tulip_giant`, `habit_hemlock`, all `context_*`), `sheets2/bark_a.png`, enlarged crops, and eight extra renders I made in `shots/extra-immersion/` (`oct_low` 16.6 h, `oct_1780` 17.8 h, `mist_october`, `rain_grove`, `fair_vista`, and three more uncurated spots `spot_5..7`). Pixel regions are (x-range, y-range) in 1600x900 for views and 1280x720 for portraits.

## 2. What works

- **The floor.** `floor.png`, `spot_5`, `spot_7`, `context_blueberry`: recognisable chestnut, maple and oak litter, burs, Christmas fern, sedge tufts. This is the one layer where I believe the place. No stipple at 300%.
- **Looking up.** `context_redoak`, `context_tulip`, `context_chestnut`: tall grey boles, a gold ceiling, sun shafts. These give a real sense of height.
- **The gold canopy from above.** `canopy.png` and `fair_vista` have the brief's "immense golden canopy punctuated by islands of scarlet", now as a continuous billowing surface.
- **Mist.** `mist_october` (extra) is the best mood in the project: muted colour, trunks stepping back into haze. It is closer to Richards than the clear-weather hero.
- **Still water.** The pool in `october.png` and `stream.png` reflects trunks and crowns and carries floating leaves.
- **The chestnut stand itself.** `habit_chestnut_veteran.png` and `habit_chestnut_giant.png` (everything else hidden) are the most beautiful frames in the whole set: an open, sunlit, golden hall with heavy limbs. The full scene never shows this (see issue 4).
- **Tone.** `october.png` median luminance is now 0.237 against the painting's 0.231, and the foreground is no longer the brightest band.

### 1. The giants do not read as ancient giants

**Where:** `trunk.png`, `bark.png`, `october.png`, `spot_1`, `spot_5`, `ridge.png`, `context_beech.png`.

**What is wrong.**
- **Cone, not column.** In `trunk.png` (650–960, 180–900) the bole tapers in a straight line from ground to a thin Y fork (800–830, 180–200). It looks like a young tree scaled up.
- **Tent skirt with blades.** The base is a smooth cone ending in a hem, with thin black fins poking out: `bark.png` (900–1040, 560–830) and (0–130, 560–760); `spot_1` (520–560, 520–580) and (990–1030, 520–575). On slopes the hem hangs clear of the ground: `spot_5` (110–520, 395–500).
- **Bark is a print.** `bark.png` (300–860, 60–460) is soft black dashes on tan with no relief and a smooth silhouette; `ridge.png` (1190–1500, 0–900) is near-black tiger stripe. I count about 25–30 dark streaks across the visible face in `bark.png`; the trunks in `chestnut-pic.png` show roughly twice as many ridges across a similar width, finer and interlaced. Coarse, blurry bark makes a 2 m bole look like a 60 cm one seen close.
- **A ring of foliage round the waist.** The hero chestnut in `october.png` has an orange spray wrapped round it at (1265–1490, 240–360), in every weather variant. Same on `context_dogwood` (490–620, 420–480) and `context_ybirch` (1060–1250, 480–540). A leaf sprig is stuck to bare bark in `bark.png` (505–590, 365–435).
- **Moss is a sock.** A uniform green band to one height all round: `habit_hemlock` (320–560, 520–720), `october.png` (0–180, 520–600).
- **No history.** No burl, hollow, scar, broken limb, lean or sweep on any bole I saw. `context_beech` (820–1230, 0–700) shows flat facets like an obelisk.
- **Never seen whole.** No eye-level curated view shows a giant from root flare to first limb.

**Reference.** `chestnut-pic.png`: columns with almost no taper for 10 m and more, a lobed flare whose roots grip the ground with moss on their tops, long braided flat-topped ridges that break the outline. `massive-branching-trunk-with-person.png`: limbs as thick as ordinary trees. `chestnut-trunk-with-group.png`: a basal hollow big enough for a boy.

**Suggestions.**
- Profile: flare that decays within 2–3 m, 5–8 angular lobes that continue into the ground as roots; then at most 8–10% taper up to the first limb at 45–60% of height; split into 2–4 limbs each carrying 40–60% of the parent's cross-section; 1–3 degrees of lean and slight out-of-round.
- Sink the base 30–50 cm into the terrain and pile litter against it. Delete the fins.
- Scale bark in world units (ridge spacing 4–8 cm whatever the girth). On the nearest LOD displace the ridges or use parallax so the silhouette breaks; grey ridge tops, dark warm furrows with occlusion.
- Forbid leaf sprays below about 60% of height on big canopy trees and reject any neighbour's leaf cluster that intersects a trunk cylinder.
- Give one giant in four a feature: burl, basal hollow, dead stub, scar, scarlet creeper up the bole (it is in the brief and in the painting).
- Mask moss with noise, biased to the upslope/north side and root tops.

### 2. Weather and time of day break the world instead of varying it

**Where:** `rain_october`, `rain_grove` (extra), `afterrain_stream`, `overcast_october`, `oct_1780` (extra), `dusk_october`, `night_grove`, `dawn_grove`, `golden_grove`, `oct_low` (extra), `golden_vista`.

**What is wrong.**
- **Rain bleaches the trunks.** The hero bole (1300–1450, 380–470) goes from luminance 0.16 in clear weather to 0.34 in rain while the litter beside it gets darker (0.24 to 0.17). The `stream` giant goes 0.075 to 0.27 after rain. In `rain_october` and `rain_grove` every trunk is a chalk-white column under dark maroon leaves, and the hemlocks frost over again (`rain_grove` 1080–1400, 0–330). A viewer sees a dead, ash-covered forest. Real wet bark goes nearly black and wet litter goes darker and richer.
- **Overcast and blue hour do the same, more mildly** (`overcast_october`, `oct_1780`: pale grey-blue boles, ice-blue boulder).
- **Dusk is a black frame.** `dusk_october` (18.2 h): median luminance 0.044, 42% of pixels below 4%. No western glow, no silhouettes against a bright sky. It is darker than `night_grove` at 19.5 h (0.077, a different view), so the evening seems to dip to black and then recover.
- **Night is a dimmed day.** `night_grove`: reds and oranges keep their hue, and the sky between leaves is bright white-blue specks (400–1100, 0–130). No moon, no stars, no blue desaturation.
- **Dawn and late afternoon look like noon.** `dawn_grove` (7.2 h) is harsh and fully saturated with no low warm light, no long shadows and no mist. `oct_low` (16.6 h) is indistinguishable from `noon_october`: mean red/blue ratio 2.03 against 2.11.
- **Golden hour has no gold.** `golden_grove` is a flat orange tint with no raking light, and the stream turns white like snow (1355–1600, 670–730, luminance 0.82). `golden_vista` is muddy olive under a pink sky: no warm rim on the crowns, no long shadows, no valley haze.

**Suggestions.**
- Wetness should multiply bark and litter albedo by roughly 0.5–0.6 and lower roughness; it must not add diffuse sky light. Apply canopy occlusion to ambient specular on bark. Make trunks, leaf cards and hemlock use the same fog and ambient path (at present trunks whiten and leaves do not).
- Drive sun colour and strength from elevation so that below 25 degrees the light is visibly warm, shadows are long and shafts appear; pool mist along the stream at dawn.
- After sunset keep skylight with a warm western band and cool zenith and let exposure follow, so dusk is dim but readable. At night desaturate towards blue, keep the sky darker than moonlit leaves, add moon and stars.
- Water must reflect an environment captured under the canopy (or screen-space reflections with that as fallback), not the open sky.

### 3. The palette is all chroma and no neutral

**Where:** `october.png`, `gap.png`, `ridge.png`, `stream.png`, `bark.png`, `cascade.png`, `spot_3`, `spot_7`.

**What is wrong.**
- Pixels with saturation above 0.8: Richards 2%; `october` 9%, `ridge` 10%, `floor` 14%, `gap` 22%, `spot_7` 29%.
- **Lemon yellow** leaf clusters with no shading read as sticky notes: `october.png` (105–450, 60–400), `stream.png` foreground. Their lit pixels sit at hue 49, saturation 0.86, value 0.91; the painting's brightest passages sit at hue 32, saturation 0.43, value 0.65.
- **Magenta-leaning crimson** blueberry: `ridge.png` (410–750, 680–900), hue 349 where the foliage in `scarlet-autumn-foliage.png` sits near hue 10.
- **Candy pink** hobblebush: `bark.png` (0–720, 280–780). **Lime** discs like lily pads: `cascade.png` (600–1600, 640–900).
- Almost no bronze, russet, tan or olive in the crowns, though the brief asks for chestnuts "golden yellow / amber / bronze" holding brown leaves, and oaks should be russet. Richards' picture is mostly olive-gold and brown with red accents. (The build log reports chestnut at 55% of canopy against the brief's 35%, which also narrows the range.)
- Evergreens are dark, structureless masses. Hemlock reads as a broadleaf clump, not a feathery conifer (`context_hemlock`, `habit_hemlock`); rhododendron and laurel are near-black holes (`stream.png` 880–1200, 400–640; 3% of that frame is below 4% luminance).
- Leaf cards are evenly lit front and back, so foliage masses have no light side, shade side or backlit glow.

**Suggestions.**
- Bring yellows down from lemon to gold and amber: hue 38–46, saturation 0.55–0.7, and a leaf albedo that cannot reach value 0.9 in ordinary light. Pull reds from magenta back to scarlet (hue 5–15). Mix bronze, brown and olive per tree and per leaf. Make 20–30% of oak crowns russet or brown-green and leave some crowns partly green. Pink to wine and maroon. Galax and ginger to dark glossy green and burgundy.
- Shade leaves two-sided with transmission, per-leaf normal and hue jitter, and occlusion inside clusters.
- Rebuild hemlock sprays as fine, flat, drooping lace with sky showing through; lift evergreen values to deep green with visible structure.
- Grade with a filmic curve that desaturates highlights. Target: no more than 3–4% of pixels above 0.8 saturation in the hero view.

### 4. Eye level is crowded and planted, and it hides the big trees

**Where:** `october.png`, `gap.png`, `ravine.png`, `stream.png`, `spot_2`, `spot_3`, `spot_1`, `grove.png`; compare `habit_chestnut_veteran.png`.

**What is wrong.**
- Sightlines stop at 15–30 m in a wall of saplings and shrubs at one even density. The brief says pre-blight understories were "more open". Hiding everything but the chestnuts (`habit_chestnut_veteran`) produces the cathedral the owner is after; the full scene buries it.
- **Repetition.** `spot_3` has six or more small red and orange trees with the same flat pagoda tiers.
- **Rod branches.** Saplings are straight pipes with kinked straight branches: `spot_1` (520–1160, 30–570); a rectangular frame of branches in `context_ybirch` (380–560, 480–570).
- **Bedded ferns.** Same-size rosettes at even spacing in `grove.png` (150–1150, 600–900); a seed-tray of identical single fronds in `spot_3` (300–1100, 430–760).
- **Pole forest.** `spot_2`, `spot_3` and `spot_7` show only 30–60 cm stems. A visitor dropped at random sees a giant in about two spots out of seven.

**Suggestions.**
- Cut sapling and shrub density away from the stream by half or more and place what remains in noise-driven clumps with open litter aisles between them; aim for 60–100 m views between boles.
- Vary sapling form: lean towards gaps, sweep, forks, different tier counts; curve and taper every branch.
- Give fern colonies a ragged edge, a density falloff and a 3:1 size range.
- Keep every eye-level curated camera clear of foliage within 3 m unless it is the subject.

### 5. `october.png` has Richards' cast but not his light

**Where:** `october.png`, `noon_october`, `oct_low`, against `richards-october.png`.

**Now right:** pool in front, great chestnut at right, pale-ish boulder at centre, red dogwood leaning as a diagonal across the chestnut, red canopy over the top, closed sky, matching key.

**Still wrong.**
- **Light.** The painting is lit low and warm from behind-left: a glowing orange vanishing point, sun on the boulder top and left trunks, a luminous green-gold shrub at the centre, deep shade over the pool. The render has no readable sun at all, at noon or at 16.6 h.
- **The centre is inverted.** Where the painting is brightest, the render has its darkest mass: hemlock and rhododendron (700–1000, 190–600).
- **No path and no glow.** The eye is led to a pale cascade (920–1000, 505–560) and white birch stems, with cold sky at (1200–1260, 150–200).
- **The chestnut** is a cone leaving the frame with a foliage ring; the painting's forks at about 4 m into two great limbs with a broken stub.
- **The boulder** (520–790, 500–640) is one soft grey lump like a tarp with a tree growing out of it; the painting has a sharp, lichen-spotted pair catching the light.
- **Foreground.** A featureless dark spindle (1095–1600, 625–720) lies across the right bank; the pool is a wide flat sheet filling the bottom third with no stones breaking it.
- **Colour.** 9% of pixels above 0.8 saturation against 2%; the lemon sapling at left is the loudest thing in the frame.
- **Format.** Still 16:9 wide, which shortens the trees.

**Suggestions.** Put the sun 15–25 degrees high behind-left with warm haze and a shaft or two; thin the hemlock behind the boulder so a lit gap closes the vista; replace the cascade end-stop with a bare-earth path or lit glade; use a chestnut that forks inside the frame; give the boulder facets, lichen and a lit top; shrink the pool to the bottom 15–20% with dark wet stones; remove or texture the spindle log; move the lemon sapling. Offer a 4:5 capture of this view. `mist_october` already shows the depth this view can have.

### 6. Moving water and the channel

**Where:** `cascade.png`, `golden_grove`, `dawn_grove`, `october.png`, `stream.png`, `context_ybirch`.

- Rainbow blotches (pink, cyan, yellow) on the fall face: `cascade.png` (570–780, 475–550). This was reported in round 1.
- The fall is a smeared translucent sheet with no foam, strands or spray.
- White-out at low sun (issue 2); a flat yellow ribbon in `context_ybirch` (1000–1280, 690–720).
- The channel is a constant-width flat-bottomed sheet. No stone breaks the surface, no pool-and-riffle, no undercut bank. Moss rocks are identical pills: `cascade.png` (345–660, 530–595) and (620–810, 400–475). Pebbles are uniform ovals on a dark band.

**Suggestions.** Clamp or remove the refraction term that makes the colours. Build falls from foam strands over a dark wet rock face. Scatter emergent stones and cobbles of mixed size and shape, vary width and depth, add leaf packs behind stones.

### 7. Dead wood and ground contact read as seams and pipes

**Where:** `grove.png` and all its variants, `spot_3`, `spot_4`, `spot_2`, `spot_1`, `floor.png`, `october.png`, `ravine.png`.

- A ruler-straight dark line runs about 10 m across the slope in `grove.png` (225–500, 430–590). It reads as a terrain seam in all six frames taken from that view.
- Logs are thin, straight, untapered and untextured: `spot_3` (230–990, 440–580) and (1255–1600, 670–860); `spot_4` (0–1000, 530–625); `spot_2` (950–1215, 715–865); a box beam in `floor.png` (1185–1600, 15–55).
- Only `gap.png` has a fallen bole of old-growth size, and its root plate is three flat planks (60–230, 350–540). The only snags I found are bare poles like fence posts in `habit_hemlock` (880–1150, 350–440).
- `ravine.png` (900–1600, 480–900): same-size leaves scattered on near-black ground read as confetti on a carpet. Leaves never drift against boles, logs or rocks.

**Suggestions.** Give logs taper, bends, breaks, branch stubs, sag into the ground, bark that sloughs, moss and decay classes; make a fifth of them 0.8–1.5 m across. Build snags as broken-topped boles of real girth. Pile litter against obstacles and thicken the base litter layer wherever loose leaf cards are sparse.

### 8. The far field in `vista.png` and `canopy.png`

- A flat grey hole in the canopy with white trunk lines: `vista.png` (875–1130, 545–645), also in `golden_vista` and `fair_vista`.
- Backdrop hills are a blurred quilt with a smooth outline and a hard join to the real trees (`vista.png` 0–1600, 300–400); in `canopy.png` (0–1600, 0–95) the texture is stretched into streaks with a sharp V notch. The same hill shows through the trees as a pale dune in `spot_2` (930–1010, 235–275).
- No aerial perspective between the cove and the first ridge.
- The land is low and rolling. A cove should feel enclosed by steep slopes.

**Suggestions.** Render ground and understory proxies under canopy gaps at distance. Give the first ridge crown-scale bumps on its silhouette and a blend zone. Fade saturation and contrast with distance. Raise and steepen the enclosing slopes.

## 4. Polish

1. Leaf cards show a faint linen weave and jagged serrations up close (`floor.png` 400–800, 560–800; `spot_6`).
2. Cream-white chestnut leaves in the `floor.png` foreground hover above the litter with no contact shadow.
3. Fern fronds laid across the log in `gap.png` (160–780, 390–500) look like decals; white specks sit on the boulder (1060–1600, 760–900).
4. `gap.png` needs an actual opening: sky, a pool of sunlight, the fallen bole as subject.
5. `ridge.png` should show the ground falling away and blue ridges between boles.
6. `trunk.png`: shoot from 2–3 m at about 60 degrees so the flare is in frame.
7. Tulip-poplar giants are bare poles with a tuft (`habit_tulip_giant`); fine in the forest, weak wherever they are seen whole.
8. No cloud shadows on the canopy in `fair_vista`.
9. Rain streaks are a few long hairlines; add a nearer, denser layer, drips and rings on the pool.
10. Pink and salmon leaves in the litter (`ravine.png`) should go to red-brown.
11. Mist in `mist_grove` leaves far foliage fully yellow while trunks fade.
12. Nothing gives human scale. A deer, a turkey or a worn trail would do it without breaking the period.

## 5. Round-1 follow-up

The four items round 1 labelled blockers, then its other ranked changes.

1. **Shadow stipple (blocker): fixed.** No dot lattice at 300% anywhere I looked.
2. **Water shading (blocker): partly.** Still water reflects and reads as water; rainbow blotches remain on the cascade, the surface whites out at low sun, falls are still a flat sheet.
3. **Far field (blocker): partly.** Scales, extruded blocks and flat polygons are gone and blue ridges layer nicely; quilt hills, smooth outline, hard join, streaks in `canopy.png` and the grey hole remain.
4. **Giant boles (blocker): mostly not fixed.** Flare is wider and mossed; still cones on a tent skirt with fins, stripe bark without relief, thin Y fork, no character; the foliage ring is new.
5. **Light and grade: partly.** Key now matches the painting and the blue fog is gone; no low warm directional light, saturation still about four times the painting's.
6. **Re-stage `october.png`: partly.** Leaning dogwood, boulder, reflecting pool and closed canopy are in; no path or glow, no fork, pool still fills a third, still 16:9.
7. **Hemlock: partly.** Frosting gone in sun; still broadleaf-looking dark clumps, and the frosting returns in rain and at blue hour.
8. **Close the canopy, enlarge crowns: partly.** Good from above and about 15% sky from below; limbs still thin under puff foliage.
9. **Open and de-regularise the understory: not fixed.**
10. **Deadwood and disturbance: partly.** One good fallen bole and many logs; most are pipes, snags are poles, no branch litter.
11. **Stream channel and rocks: partly.** Flagstones replaced by pebbles and a pale boulder added; constant flat channel and identical moss pills remain.
12. **Camera standard: partly.** `ravine` and `grove` are clear; `stream` and `gap` still have foliage in the lens; `bark` still frames the weakest material.
13. **Floor relief: partly.** Richer litter and clustered burs; cards still flat and coplanar, no drifts, roots or hummocks.
14. **Atmosphere in motion: partly, as far as stills show.** Shafts in `grove` and the look-ups, clouds in fair weather; no moon or stars at night, and no sun disc or glow in any forest-interior view.
15. **Species asset and capture fixes: partly.** Giants have their own frames and I saw no hairline; candy-pink hobblebush and pale discs remain.
